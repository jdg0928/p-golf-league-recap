# Monday Men's League — 2026 Season Recap

## Fourteen Mondays, one rainout, and a lot of golf

The 2026 season ran the full 15-week calendar with a single exception: Week 12 washed out and went unplayed, with no makeup. That left 14 Mondays of golf, and the group filled them well. There were 334 rounds in all, close to 24 players on the sheet in an average week, and 3,006 holes played across the alternating front and back nines. Add it up and the league finished roughly 3,480 strokes over par for the summer.

## Two aces in two weeks

The season's biggest news arrived before anyone had settled in. Joey Desimone holed his tee shot on the 12th in Week 1, playing in the 6 p.m. group. A week later, Dennis McNicholl did the same thing on the 9th in the 5:40 p.m. group. Two holes-in-one in the opening fortnight, and the first anyone can remember in league play in at least a decade.

Both show up in the season's scoring data, too. The league made 10 eagles or better all year, and two of them were these. For Joey Desimone, the ace was the only eagle he made all season.

## The points race

Points came every week: 10 for the win, then 8-7-6-5-4-3-2 down the board, and a point for everyone else who played. Ties went to the USGA system.

Jason Gerdom took the Season Points Race with 67 points, built on the only perfect attendance in the league, 14 starts out of 14. Logan Slattery finished second at 58, and Jim Madden was third at 55.

The efficiency story sits just underneath the standings. Jim Madden earned his 55 points in only eight appearances, a rate of nearly seven points a round, the best in the league by a wide margin. Austin Isaacson was close behind at better than five points a start across nine rounds. The title came down to a familiar truth: showing up every week matters, and Jason Gerdom was the one who did.

Jason Gerdom took the Ringer Scorecard as well, finishing at 48, which is 22 under par across the full 18. Jim Madden led that one for the first six weeks before Jason Gerdom took it over for good.

## The weekly contests

The one-off contests gave the season its texture, and 28 prizes went out across the summer.

The Weekly Reload, low net for the night, ran every week and paid a sleeve of Titleist ProV1s. Jim Madden won it three times, more than anyone, including the very first Monday. David Hodge, Dan Cuddy, and Jeff Janczak took two apiece, and Brian Zinn, Logan Slattery, Robert Huften, Austin Isaacson, and Christof Marre each grabbed one.

On the skill contests, Logan Slattery made an 8-foot, 4-inch putt to win Longest Putt in Week 6, Brandon Bogrette answered with 15 feet, 7 inches in Week 10, and Trent Gutstein closed it out in Week 15. Closest to the Pin produced the season's two funniest numbers on the same night: in Week 14, Steve Menges won the first-shot prize from 42 feet, 4 inches, because nobody troubled the flag, while Trent Gutstein took the second-shot prize from 2 feet, 7 inches, the closest anyone hit it all season. Steven Cruz and Jim Madden split the Week 9 prizes, and Logan Slattery and Steve Menges split Week 11.

The World Cup Challenge made its debut in Week 8 and went to David Hodge, who piled up five wins and two draws against net par for 17 points. Patrick Healy won Circle 5 in Week 13 at five under net on his five chosen holes, and Andrew Walker took the Wrong 5 alternative prize at plus one.

Sixteen of the 26 players in the contest division won at least one prize this season.

## Where the strokes went

Because the league plays nine at a time, the full 18 only comes together over the course of a summer. Laid end to end, the two nines are nearly identical in difficulty: the front plays about 10.46 strokes over par as a group, the back 10.39.

Inside that, the course is anything but even. The par-5 2nd was the hardest hole on the property at 6.72, and it gave up exactly one birdie all season. The 17th was next at 5.66, a closer where more than half of all scores were double bogey or worse. The 5th ranked third despite being the shortest of the three par 5s on the front, with nearly half its scores at double or worse.

At the other end, the short par-3 4th was the only hole on the course that played under four strokes, at 3.84. The 8th was the birdie hole with 16, more than any hole on the card, and the 6th produced 12 birdies and exactly as many pars as bogeys. The steadiest hole was the 13th, which yielded 71 pars, more than any other.

Two holes deserve footnotes. The 3rd is a two-tiered green, and the pin sat on the harder back shelf every week the league played the front, which helps explain why a short par 3 ranked fourth-hardest on the course. And the 11th played closer to a par 3½ for much of the season while crews put in new drainage and a retaining pond, so its scoring average is not comparable to the rest of the card. That work is finished.

The quiet villain was the par-3 12th. It produced 88 bogeys, more than any hole on the course. Seldom a par, seldom a disaster, almost always a bogey.

## The best sticks

Measured by raw gross scoring, Logan Slattery was the league's most reliable ball-striker at 37.8 a round, with Trent Gutstein (38.3) and David Boardman (38.5) right behind. Logan Slattery converted it, too, finishing second in points. He was the season's metronome: 62 pars, more than anyone else by a comfortable margin, par or better on two-thirds of the holes he played, and only two holes of triple bogey or worse all year.

Not every hot hand found the winner's circle. Joey Desimone had the fourth-lowest gross average and tied for the most birdies in the league at 14, plus that hole-in-one, yet finished 23rd in points. Every league has a player like this, and this year it was Joey Desimone.

## Consistency versus fireworks

Two players made the case for opposite temperaments and arrived at the same place. Logan Slattery won with steadiness. Trent Gutstein won with range: he hit a league-high three eagles and, remarkably, did not make a single triple bogey or worse across 108 holes. Austin Isaacson matched the clean card, going his whole season without a triple as well. Big numbers without the blow-ups is a hard combination to pull off, and a few players managed it this year.

## The heart of the league

The recap does not belong only to the low scorers. Brandon Bogrette led the league in triple bogeys or worse, about four a round, and still banked 34 points for a share of ninth, because net scoring kept him in the fight every week. Bob Fitzgerald had the roughest scoring line of anyone, reaching par or better on just 6% of his holes, and answered the bell 13 of 14 Mondays anyway. That is as much the league as anything on the leaderboard.

Net scoring is what makes that possible across a field this wide. Jim Madden is the proof: one of the higher gross averages in the league at 50.6, and the best net average of anyone at 33.6, a swing of about 17 strokes a round. He also posted the lowest single net round of the year, a 30.

## By the numbers

Across all 3,006 holes, the bogey was king, showing up 34% of the time. Par or better came in at 30%. The season produced 118 birdies and 10 eagles or better. It is a fair snapshot of a recreational nine-hole league: a lot of bogey golf, the occasional red number to keep it interesting, and enough good rounds spread around to make the standings worth chasing.

## Looking ahead

The core of the format held up again this year. Net scoring bridged a wide handicap spread, attendance stayed strong, and the points race went down to the wire. With the World Cup Challenge now in the mix and more one-off contests on the way, 2027 has plenty to build on.
