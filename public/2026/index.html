The standings

The points race

Points every week: 10 for the win, then 8-7-6-5-4-3-2 down the board, and a point for everyone else who plays. Ties broken by the USGA system. The title went to the man who never missed a Monday, but the best rate per start belonged to the players who played the least.

Under construction
No. 11 played closer to a par 3½ for much of the season while crews put in new drainage and a retaining pond. It was still scored to its par 4 on the card, so its average is not comparable to the other 17 holes. The work is finished, so 2027 should give a truer read.
The back-pin par 3
No. 3 is a two-tiered green, and the pin sat on the back shelf every week the league played the front. It is the tougher of the two locations by a wide margin, and the hole ranked 4th-hardest on the course.
The card-wrecker
Par-3 No. 12 quietly did the most damage of all — 88 bogeys, more than any other hole on the course. Seldom a par, seldom a disaster, almost always a bogey.
Even halves
The two nines play almost identically: the front runs +10.46 over par as a group, the back +10.39. Whichever side you played, the course asked about the same of you.
